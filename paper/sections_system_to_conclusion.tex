\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system}

We consider a federated learning (FL) system with $n$ clients $\mathcal{C}=\{c_1,\dots,c_n\}$ and one
aggregator $\Agg$ (the server). Client $c_i$ holds a private labelled dataset
$D_i=\{(x,y)\}$ whose label set $\mathcal{Y}_i$ is \emph{its own}: clients may hold different datasets
(e.g., MNIST, EMNIST, CIFAR-10), so label sets can overlap ($\mathcal{Y}_i\cap\mathcal{Y}_j\neq\emptyset$),
be disjoint, and differ in size (\emph{label-heterogeneous} FL). Following GeFL, clients
collaboratively train a \emph{conditional generator} $G(z,k)$ and $\Agg$ trains a global classifier
$F$ on synthetic samples; clients share no classifier, so $F$ covers every class without any client
holding all of them.

A label is identified by a \emph{keyword} typed by its owner. In the \emph{exact} setting all owners of
a class use the same name; in the \emph{fuzzy} setting owners use their own wording
(e.g., ``car'' vs.\ ``Car'', or different synonyms), and no shared dictionary exists.
Let $\mathcal{U}=\bigcup_i \mathcal{Y}_i$ be the union of classes with $U=|\mathcal{U}|$.
A public bulletin board (BB) relays broadcast messages.

The generator is a class-conditional-BatchNorm (CBN) DCGAN: a shared \emph{trunk} $\theta_T$ and one
parameter \emph{row} $\theta_k\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$ per class $k$ (label embedding and per-layer
$\gamma,\beta$; $P=912$). A client holding class $k$ needs only $\theta_T$ and $\theta_k$.
The goal is to train $\theta_T$, $\{\theta_k\}$ and $F$ such that $F$ classifies all
classes of all clients, while protecting \emph{which client holds which label}.

\section{Threat Model and Design Goals}
\label{sec:threat}

All parties are semi-honest (honest-but-curious); $\Agg$ does not collude with clients.
We require:
\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{G1}] \textbf{Label privacy against $\Agg$}: $\Agg$ learns neither the label names nor
        which client holds which label.
  \item[\textbf{G2}] \textbf{Label privacy among clients}: a client learns nothing about other clients'
        labels beyond $U$, and in particular not how many clients hold its own labels.
  \item[\textbf{G3}] \textbf{Row confidentiality}: only holders of class $k$ obtain $\theta_k$.
  \item[\textbf{G4}] \textbf{Update confidentiality}: $\Agg$ learns only aggregates of client updates.
  \item[\textbf{G5}] \textbf{Efficiency}: per-round traffic comparable to non-private FL.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Accepted leakage.} $\Agg$ learns $U$, the global model, and, per round, the \emph{number} of
contributors to each row (never who); this is inherent to taking a per-class mean and is discussed
in Sec.~\ref{sec:security}.

\section{Proposed Scheme}
\label{sec:scheme}

Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} \todo{overview figure} shows the protocol. It has a one-time
\emph{setup} (private label union and key derivation) followed by FL \emph{rounds}
(label-private downlink, local training, compressed secure aggregation, server training).

\subsection{Private Label Union}
\label{sec:union}

\emph{Tags.} Each client draws a key share $k_i$; the joint OPRF key $K=\prod_i k_i$ exists nowhere.
For each own label $x$, $c_i$ sends the blinded point $H(x)\cdot r$ around a ring of clients, each
multiplying by its share, and finally removes $r$, obtaining the tag $T(x)=H(x)\cdot K$ on
ristretto255. Equal labels yield equal tags; every hop sees uniformly random group elements, and lists
are padded to the maximum list size $m$, so no client learns another's label count.

\emph{Union by secure aggregation.} Each client writes, for each tag, a pair
$(r,\ r\cdot\mathrm{chk}(T))$ with fresh uniform $r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2^{64}}$ into bucket
$b(T)=\mathrm{SHA\text{-}256}(T)\bmod 2^{20}$ of a vector; the vectors are summed by SecAgg~[Bonawitz].
Occupied buckets form the union; the index of a label is the rank of its bucket. Because a sum of
uniform values is uniform, the number of holders is hidden. Bucket collisions are detected by the
checksum and resolved by re-hashing with a public salt.

\emph{Keys.} Each holder derives $sk_k=\mathrm{KDF}(T)$ and $pk_k=sk_k\cdot B$ locally. $\Agg$ obtains
$pk_0,\dots,pk_{U-1}$ through a second SecAgg in which every holder of $k$ writes
$(r,\ r\cdot c_j)$ for the 16-bit chunks $c_j$ of $pk_k$; with $A=\sum r=2^s a'$ ($a'$ odd) the
chunks are recovered as $c_j=(B_j/2^s)\,a'^{-1}\bmod 2^{64-s}$, while $A$ itself is uniform and
hides the holder count.

\emph{Fuzzy union (no dictionary).} Without shared names, each client first maps its keyword to a
canonical key \emph{locally}, using only public data: (i) text normalization (NFKC, lower-case,
numerals spelled out; single letters keep their case), (ii) embedding by a public cross-lingual
sentence encoder, (iii) snapping to the nearest word of a public vocabulary (the $N$ most frequent
English words) under the CSLS score $2\cos(x,a)-r_a$, where $r_a$ is the mean similarity of anchor $a$
to its $h$ nearest anchors (suppressing ``hub'' words); a client skips anchors that are more frequent
in its own language than in English (false friends). The anchor identifier then enters the exact
OPRF union unchanged. Hence fuzzy matching adds \emph{no} leakage: all similarity computation is local
and against public data. The parameters $(N,h)$ are calibrated once on public class names by the
Matthews correlation (MCC) of the resulting grouping.

\subsection{Label-Private Downlink}
In each round $\Agg$ broadcasts $\theta_T$ and, for every index $k$, a KEM ciphertext of $\theta_k$
under $pk_k$ (DHKEM on ristretto255 + AES-GCM, HPKE-style, AAD = (round, $k$)). Every index receives
a ciphertext of equal size, so readers cannot count active labels; only holders of $sk_k$ open row $k$.

\subsection{Local Training}
Client $c_i$ trains its generator (trunk and own rows) against a private local discriminator for $E_g$
epochs. A client relabels once after setup: its local label $a$ uses row $\mathrm{idx}_i(a)$; because
conditioning is a row lookup, a generator warmed up locally \emph{before} the union can be remapped
without retraining (optional warm-up of $E_w$ epochs).

\subsection{Compressed Secure Aggregation}
Every client uploads one fixed-length vector (zeros where it has nothing, so participation leaks
nothing):
\begin{align*}
  v_i=\big[\,&q(\Delta\theta_T[\mathcal{K}]),\ q(\Delta\theta_0[\mathcal{R}]),\dots,
  q(\Delta\theta_{U-1}[\mathcal{R}]),\\
  &\mathbf{1}_{T},\ \mathbf{1}_0,\dots,\mathbf{1}_{U-1},\ \rho_i\,\big],
\end{align*}
where $\mathcal{K},\mathcal{R}$ are public per-round random coordinate subsets of fraction
$\kappa$ (the same for all clients), $q(\cdot)$ is 8-bit stochastic rounding
$q(u)=\mathrm{round}_{\mathrm{stoch}}(127\,\mathrm{clip}(u/s,-1,1))$ with public per-tensor scales $s$,
$\mathbf{1}_k$ indicates a contribution to row $k$, and $\rho_i$ are per-tensor clip fractions.
Since $|q|\le127$, the sum of $n\le258$ clients fits in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^{16}}$, so SecAgg runs with a
16-bit modulus (2 bytes per coordinate instead of 8). $\Agg$ recovers
$\sum_i v_i$ and updates
\[
  \theta_k \leftarrow \theta_k - \frac{s_k}{127\,n_k}\sum_i q_i(\Delta\theta_k)
  \quad\text{if } n_k=\textstyle\sum_i \mathbf{1}_k \ge t,
\]
i.e., the unbiased mean over contributors, with a minimum of $t$ holders (default $t=2$).
\emph{Adaptive clipping}: from the aggregated clip fractions $\bar\rho$ (again only a sum), the scale
of each tensor block is multiplied by $4$ if $\bar\rho>10\%$, by $2$ if $\bar\rho>1\%$ and by $0.9$
if $\bar\rho<0.1\%$, which keeps clipping near zero without revealing individual updates.

\subsection{Server Training}
$\Agg$, which holds all rows, samples $G(z,k)$ for every index $k$ and trains the global classifier $F$
(ResNet-18) for $E_s$ epochs per round. Algorithm~\ref{alg:protocol} summarizes the protocol.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Label-private generative FL}
\label{alg:protocol}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Setup} (once)
\State each $c_i$: (fuzzy only) keyword $\to$ normalize $\to$ embed $\to$ CSLS-snap to public anchor
\State OPRF ring $\Rightarrow$ tags $T(x)$; SecAgg bucket union $\Rightarrow$ indices $\mathrm{idx}_i$, $U$
\State $sk_k=\mathrm{KDF}(T)$; SecAgg of $pk_k$ chunks $\Rightarrow$ $\Agg$ learns $pk_0..pk_{U-1}$
\State (optional) local warm-up $E_w$ epochs; remap local rows to indices
\For{round $r=1,\dots,R$}
  \State $\Agg$: broadcast $\theta_T$; post $\mathrm{Enc}_{pk_k}(\theta_k)$ for all $k$
  \State each $c_i$: open own rows; train $G$ vs.\ local $D$ for $E_g$ epochs
  \State each $c_i$: $v_i\gets$ keep-$\kappa$, 8-bit quantize, indicators, clip fractions
  \State $\Agg$: $\sum_i v_i\gets\mathrm{SecAgg}_{2^{16}}(\{v_i\})$; contributor mean if $n_k\ge t$
  \State $\Agg$: adapt scales; train $F$ on $G(z,k)$ for $E_s$ epochs
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

\emph{G1 (Aggregator).} $\Agg$ sees (a) SecAgg sums, (b) public keys, (c) aggregated masked updates.
In the union, the sum per bucket is uniform in $\mathbb{Z}_{2^{64}}$ whenever at least one client
occupies it, so $\Agg$ learns only which buckets are occupied, i.e., $U$; bucket positions are
pseudorandom functions of tags under a key $\Agg$ does not hold, so indices reveal nothing about names.
$pk_k$ is aggregated with uniform masks, hiding holders and holder counts. Uploads are fixed-length
dense vectors from every client, so a client's label set is not revealed by its message.

\emph{G2 (clients).} Ring hops see uniformly random group elements (DDH) and padded lists. The union
output (occupied buckets) reveals $U$ only. Fuzzy matching is local against public data and does not
change the transcript.

\emph{G3.} Row $k$ is encrypted under $pk_k$ and every index gets an equal-size ciphertext, so
non-holders learn neither $\theta_k$ nor which rows are in use.

\emph{G4.} By SecAgg, $\Agg$ learns only $\sum_i v_i$. Compression is applied \emph{before} masking and
the masks are uniform modulo $2^{16}$, so the 16-bit ring hides values exactly as the 64-bit one; the
public coordinate subsets and scales carry no client information.

\emph{Residual leakage.} $\Agg$ learns $U$ and, per round, $n_k$ for each row, needed to compute the
mean; it never learns which clients contributed. Hiding $n_k$ as well (e.g., dividing inside
MPC/VOLE or HE) is left to future work; we note that clients do \emph{not} learn $n_k$.

\section{Complexity and Communication}
\label{sec:cost}
\emph{Setup.} The ring OPRF costs $n\cdot m$ group operations and $n\,m\cdot 32$ bytes per client; the
bucket union is one SecAgg over $2^{20}$ words; key aggregation is one SecAgg over $17U$ words.
The fuzzy mapping adds only local encoder inference.
\emph{Per round.} Upload per client is $2\big(\kappa|\theta_T| + U(\kappa P+1) + 1 + B\big)$ bytes
plus SecAgg control traffic ($B$: number of tensor blocks), versus $4(|\theta_T|+|\mathcal{Y}_i|P)$
bytes for uncompressed plaintext FL. The download is $|\theta_T|$ plus $U$ ciphertexts of size
$4P$+overhead.

\section{Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Setup}
We use $n=30$ clients: 10 each with MNIST, EMNIST (byclass, 62 classes) and CIFAR-10
($U=72$ classes; digits of MNIST and EMNIST are the same classes). Data are split by a Dirichlet
distribution with $\alpha=0.1$ (non-IID) and $\alpha=100$ (IID), seed 15698. The generator is a
CBN-DCGAN ($32\times32$), the local discriminator is a DCGAN discriminator, and the server classifier is
ResNet-18. Per round: $E_g=5$ generator epochs, $E_s=5$ server epochs on 64 synthetic samples per class;
Adam ($2\times10^{-4}$ for the GAN, $10^{-3}$ for $F$); $R=\todo{50}$ rounds, $E_w=\todo{5}$
warm-up epochs, $t=2$, $\kappa=\todo{1.0 / 0.1}$.
Fuzzy setting: two writers per class using different keyword forms; public encoder
\texttt{paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2}, $N=\todo{2000}$ anchors, CSLS $h=\todo{5}$.

\emph{Baselines.} \textbf{Plain-GeFL}: the same model and update rule without cryptography (plaintext
union by name, rows sent in the clear, identical 8-bit compression, only own rows uploaded).
\textbf{Ours (exact PSI)} and \textbf{Ours (fuzzy PSI)}: the proposed protocol with the exact and
fuzzy union.

\subsection{Label Union}
The exact union recovers all $U=72$ classes. The fuzzy union reaches MCC $=\todo{1.000}$ (precision
$\todo{1.000}$, recall $\todo{1.000}$) against the true classes, i.e., it reproduces the exact
grouping without any shared dictionary; setup takes \todo{83}\,s for 30 clients.

\subsection{Accuracy}
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) shows global accuracy per round. Ours (exact and fuzzy) matches Plain-GeFL
\todo{within X points}: since compression is identical and the aggregate is the same, the protocol
changes \emph{who learns what}, not the model update. Because the PSI-based union does not depend on
trained models, training on the shared label space starts at round~1, unlike classifier-based
label mapping, which must first train generators and classifiers (e.g., 25 rounds).

\subsection{Cost}
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b)--(c) report time per stage and traffic per client and round. With
$\kappa=\todo{0.1}$, the upload is \todo{270\,kB} per client and round versus \todo{5.0\,MB} for
uncompressed plaintext FL; the cryptographic overhead is dominated by SecAgg (\todo{s}/round), and
the one-time setup costs \todo{17\,MB} upload per client.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a label-private generative FL protocol for label-heterogeneous clients that hold
different datasets. A multi-party OPRF with a SecAgg bucket union aligns labels without revealing names, holders or
holder counts; a fuzzy variant aligns differently worded labels by local mapping to public anchors, at
no extra leakage. A CBN generator with KEM-sealed rows keeps each class's parameters with its holders,
and compressed 16-bit secure aggregation keeps the per-round traffic close to non-private FL while
matching its accuracy. Future work includes hiding per-row contributor counts from the aggregator
and extending fuzzy matching to multilingual and synonym-rich label vocabularies.
