\paragraph{Label Union by Circuit PSI.}
The first offline step builds the \emph{union list}, one entry per distinct concept held by any
client, such that labels of different clients that denote the same concept share one entry, without
revealing any $\mathcal{Y}_i$. Two labels $y_i\in\mathcal{Y}_i$ and $y_j\in\mathcal{Y}_j$ should
share an entry iff both $d_{\mathrm{sem}}(y_i,y_j)$ and $d_{\mathrm{vis}}(D_i^{(y_i)},D_j^{(y_j)})$
are small. A plain PSI would reveal which labels match; with circuit PSI the pairwise matches and the
resulting groups stay inside an MPC among the clients, and the only output is, per label, a random
group secret $K$ that is equal for all labels of one concept and is opened only to the label's owner.
The step proceeds as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Semantic description.} Each client $C_i$ describes every $y\in\mathcal{Y}_i$ by a
keyword $w_i(y)$ naming its concept, in one of two modes.
\emph{Exact mode} is used when the domain shares a public vocabulary (e.g., all clients use one
medical terminology): $w_i(y)$ is taken from a public dictionary $\mathcal{W}$ of canonical terms
with synonyms folded, and two labels match semantically iff their hashed keywords
$H_{\mathrm n}(w_i(y))$ are equal.
\emph{Similarity mode} is used when the clients do not agree on, or do not want to commit to, a
common vocabulary: keywords are written freely, a public text encoder $E$ maps them to unit vectors
$\mathbf{e}=E(w_i(y))$, and two keywords match iff
\begin{equation}
  2\langle\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e}'\rangle-r-r'\ \ge\ \tau,
  \label{eq:csls}
\end{equation}
the CSLS test, where $r$ is the mean similarity of $\mathbf{e}$ to its $h$ nearest words of a public
anchor vocabulary; the hub terms discount generic words that are close to everything.

\item \emph{Visual description.} A keyword alone cannot tell, e.g., a handwritten ``3'' from a
printed one. From a public seed every client generates the same \emph{probe set}: $N_I$ random images
$\boldsymbol\xi_1,\dots,\boldsymbol\xi_{N_I}$ and their coordinates under a public signature function
$\sigma$. For each $y$, $C_i$ computes the mean signature $\bar{\boldsymbol\sigma}_i(y)$ of
$D_i^{(y)}$ and the indicator $\mathbf{s}_i(y)\in\{0,1\}^{N_I}$ of its $k$ nearest probes. Two labels
are visually similar iff $\langle\mathbf{s}_i(y_i),\mathbf{s}_j(y_j)\rangle\ge t$, a coarse public
proxy for a small $d_{\mathrm{vis}}$; only probe indices enter the computation, never samples.

\item \emph{Secure matching and grouping.} Each label becomes a row $(v,\phi,\mathbf{s})$: a valid
bit, the keyword feature (hashed keyword or fixed-point embedding with hub term), and the
probe indicator. Dummy rows ($v=0$) pad every client to $m$ rows, hiding $|\mathcal{Y}_i|$. The rows
are Shamir-shared among the clients, which evaluate with an honest-majority MPC, for every pair of
rows $a,a'$ of different clients,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{aa'}=v_a v_{a'}\,\big[\langle\mathbf{s}_a,\mathbf{s}_{a'}\rangle\ge t\big]\,
  \mathrm{KW}(\phi_a,\phi_{a'}),
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{KW}$ keyword equality (exact mode) or test~\eqref{eq:csls}. Since matching is
not transitive, the MPC merges matching rows into connected components by min-label propagation,
each row carrying a joint random secret, and opens to each owner only the secret $K$ of its rows.
Labels of one concept now hold the same $K$; nobody sees a match bit or a group, and a random $K$
says nothing about its label.

\item \emph{Delivery by secure sum.} Each client writes, for every secret $K$ it holds, a random
nonzero $\alpha$ and a check value $\alpha\,\mathrm{chk}(K)$ into bucket $\pi(K)=H_{\mathrm b}(K)$ of
a vector $\mathbf{y}_i$ with $2^\beta$ entries, zeros elsewhere. The aggregator obtains
$\boldsymbol\Sigma=\mathsf{SecSum}(\{\mathbf{y}_i\})$ and nothing else. Holders of one concept write
into the same bucket, so the nonzero entries of $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ form the union list, and a sum of
random values hides how many clients hold each entry. The aggregator posts the occupied buckets,
sorted, on $\mathsf{BB}$; their number $L$ is the size of the union. A bucket that received two
different secrets fails $\boldsymbol\Sigma[\pi]_1=\mathrm{chk}(K)\,\boldsymbol\Sigma[\pi]_0$ at its
holders, and the step is repeated with a fresh public salt. As buckets are hashes of secrets only
their holders know, the posted list reveals no label; each client locates its own buckets in it,
which gives the index of each of its labels.
\end{enumerate}
